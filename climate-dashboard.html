Gilgit-Baltistan Climate Trends & Glacial-Melt Risk

An interactive machine-learning dashboard analysing 36 years (1984–2019) of temperature and precipitation records from seven weather stations across Gilgit-Baltistan, Pakistan — the gateway to the country's 7,000+ glaciers. It quantifies warming trends, maps them geographically, groups stations by climate behaviour, flags anomalous years, and projects future values to highlight where accelerating glacial melt threatens downstream communities.

Key insights

  • The region is warming: maximum temperature is rising by an average of +0.59 °C per decade across the seven stations.
  • Skardu warms fastest at +2.20 °C per decade — a high-elevation gateway to the Karakoram glaciers, exactly where melt matters most.
  • Elevation-dependent warming: a +0.51 correlation between elevation and trend suggests higher stations are warming faster — a recognised and dangerous mountain-climate signal.
  • Melt & flood risk: faster high-altitude warming accelerates glacial melt, swells unstable glacial lakes, and raises the risk of Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs) for downstream communities.

Explore the interactive dashboard

Try the live app in your browser: gilgit-baltistan-climate.streamlit.app. Or run it locally after cloning the repository:

python -m streamlit run climate/gb_climate_app.py

Source data parsed from Pakistan Meteorological Department station records (1984–2019). Code and data are on GitHub.

A demonstration analysis of station records using interpretable machine-learning methods — not an operational climate forecast. Station coordinates and elevations are accurate approximations for mapping. Built to show method and motivation for glacial-melt and climate-risk research in Pakistan.
